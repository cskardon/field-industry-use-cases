= Track and Trace

Pharmaceutical track and trace is the end-to-end ability to follow a product, or any of its components, through every step of the supply chain, from the raw materials delivered by a supplier to the finished goods dispensed at a pharmacy. Regulatory frameworks such as the https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-supply-chain-security-act-dscsa[Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA)] in the United States and the https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/falsified-medicines_en[Falsified Medicines Directive (FMD)] in Europe mandate this capability, requiring manufacturers, wholesalers, and dispensers to verify, capture, and share product movement data at the serialised unit level.

Despite the regulatory imperative, most organisations still rely on a combination of ERP systems, spreadsheets, and document management tools to reconstruct batch history. These point-in-time, siloed records make simple questions - _"Which finished goods batches contain raw material lot X?"_ or _"What equipment was used across the full ancestry of a recalled product?"_ - surprisingly difficult and slow to answer.

Graph databases reframe the problem. A pharmaceutical supply chain is, at its core, a network of connected entities: suppliers feed raw materials into production stages, equipment transforms those materials into batches, employees operate and certify the equipment, and distributors receive the finished product. Neo4j models this network natively, enabling multi-hop traceability queries that would require complex SQL joins across many tables to be expressed as concise, intuitive graph patterns.

== Scenario

HeartPharma Ltd manufactures *Cardioxetin*, a first-line cardiovascular treatment available in 10 mg and 20 mg film-coated tablet formulations. The product is manufactured at sites in Dublin (API synthesis and granulation), Lyon (tabletting), and Rotterdam (packaging), before being distributed to EU and North American markets.

During a routine quality review, the quality assurance team places a quality hold on a raw material batch - *LOT-RM-2024-001* - of the Cardioxetin Base Compound supplied by SynthChem GmbH. They need to answer several urgent questions:

* Which downstream batches - API, bulk granulate, drug product, and finished goods - were produced using this raw material lot?
* Which distributors received finished product containing this raw material?
* What equipment was involved in manufacturing the affected batches, and were any other products processed on the same equipment around the same time?
* Were all operators involved in those production runs fully certified for the equipment they used?
* Is the base compound sourced from more than one supplier, or does this hold create a single-supplier bottleneck that will disrupt future production?

These questions cut across batch records, production run logs, equipment maintenance records, operator certification data, and distribution records - all of which are typically held in different systems.

== Solution

Neo4j models the supply chain as a single, connected graph where suppliers, raw materials, production stages, batches, equipment, employees, and distributors are all first-class nodes linked by typed relationships. Tracing a batch from its raw material origin to a distributor becomes a single variable-length path query - no joins, no ETL, no cross-referencing separate reports.

The graph structure supports both forward tracing (which downstream products are at risk from a supplier hold?) and backward tracing (what inputs contributed to a specific finished goods lot?). Common cause analysis - finding the shared equipment, operator, or raw material batch that links a set of adverse events - is a natural multi-hop pattern that graph databases execute efficiently even across large datasets.

Some specific advantages:

* *Instant blast radius*: a single Cypher query traverses the full batch lineage tree in milliseconds, regardless of how many hops separate the raw material from the distributor.
* *Relationship-native queries*: questions about shared equipment, co-certified operators, and supply chain bypass paths are expressed directly as graph patterns rather than correlated subqueries.
* *Flexible schema*: new entities (serialised unit scans, cold-chain sensor readings, adverse event reports) can be added to the graph without schema migrations or re-engineering existing queries.

== Data Model

image::life-sciences/supply-chain/track-and-trace-model.svg[width=800]

The graph model spans two complementary layers: the *product flow layer* and the *batch-production layer*.

=== Product Flow Layer

The product flow layer describes the logical supply chain - the recipe-level path a product takes from raw material through to distribution.

* *Suppliers* provide raw materials and are connected to `RM` nodes via `SUPPLIES_RM` relationships that carry a `supplierCapacity` property.
* *RM* (Raw Material), *API* (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient), *BULK*, *DP* (Drug Product), *FG* (Finished Goods), and *DIST* (Distribution Unit) nodes represent successive manufacturing stages. All carry `PRODUCT_FLOW` relationships connecting them in sequence, with properties such as `conversionRatio`, `inPercent`, `outPercent`, `globalBrand`, `strength`, `form`, `generation`, and `market`.
* *Distributor* nodes represent regional distribution partners. The `DIST` stage connects to a `Distributor` via a `DISTRIBUTED_BY` relationship that carries the `demandQty` (units ordered).

=== Batch and Production Layer

The batch-production layer tracks the physical execution of the supply chain - specific lots, the runs that produced them, and the people and machines involved.

* *Batch* nodes represent individual production lots. They carry `lotNumber`, `materialType`, `dateProduced`, `location`, and product-identifying properties (`globalBrand`, `strength`, `form`, `generation`). Batch nodes connect to their product stage node via `IS_BATCH_FOR`, to their supplier via `SUPPLIES_BATCH`, and to downstream batches (and ultimately distributors) via `BATCH_FLOW`.
* *ProductionRun* nodes represent a single machine run that produces a batch. They carry timing information (`startTime`, `finishTime`, `runDuration`) and connect to the output batch via `PRODUCED_BATCH` and to the equipment used via `USED_EQUIP`. The chain of consecutive runs on the same equipment is captured by `NEXT_RUN_EQUIP` relationships (with `idleDuration`), and the first run on each piece of equipment is flagged by `FIRST_RUN_EQUIP`.
* *Equipment* nodes carry physical asset details (`equipID`, `nomenclature`, `manufacturer`, `model`, `serialNum`, `location`). Certified operators are linked via `IS_CERTIFIED_OPERATOR` (with `yearsExperience`).
* *Employee* nodes record the workforce. An employee can `PERFORMED_RUN`, `PERFORMED_SETUP`, or `PERFORMED_POSTRUN` on the corresponding run-stage node.
* *PreRunSetup* and *PostRunStage* nodes wrap each production run, capturing the `setupDuration` and `postRunDuration` (cleaning/sanitisation) respectively. They connect via `PREP_FOR_RUN` / `POSTRUN` to the `ProductionRun`, and via `EQUIP_SETUP` / `EQUIP_POSTRUN` to the `Equipment`. A shortened `postRunDuration` can flag a potential contamination risk between consecutive runs on the same equipment.
* *GlobalBrand* nodes anchor the product family and connect to `Recipe` nodes via `HAS_RECIPE`.

== Demo Data

The dataset models the supply chain for *Cardioxetin* (10 mg and 20 mg film-coated tablets) manufactured by HeartPharma Ltd. It includes:

* 3 suppliers, 3 raw materials, 2 API stages, 2 bulk, 2 drug product, 2 finished goods, and 2 distribution units
* 2 distributors (EU and North American markets)
* 6 production batches - one of which (LOT-RM-2024-001) is placed on quality hold to drive the recall scenario
* 3 pieces of equipment across 3 manufacturing sites
* 3 employees with varying certification profiles
* 4 production runs, 3 pre-run setups, and 3 post-run stages (one with a shortened cleaning window)

*Key insights built into the data:*

The Cardioxetin Base Compound (RM-001) is sourced exclusively from SynthChem GmbH - a single-supplier risk. Both the 10 mg and 20 mg strengths depend on this raw material for API synthesis. The Korsch Tablet Press (equip2) was used to manufacture both the 10 mg bulk granulate and the 20 mg finished goods batch, creating a potential cross-contamination vector if the suspect lot is confirmed out-of-specification.

== Cypher Queries

The queries that follow address the key supply chain questions raised by the HeartPharma quality hold scenario.

=== Full Batch Lineage from Supplier to Distributor

Given a specific raw material lot number, trace the full physical chain of custody - from the supplier who delivered it, through every intermediate batch, to the distributor who received the finished product.

This is the foundational track-and-trace query. In a traditional system it would require joining purchase order records, manufacturing execution system logs, and warehouse despatch records from three or more systems. In the graph it is a single variable-length path traversal.

[source, cypher, role=noheader]
----
// Full chain of custody for a raw material lot number
MATCH p = (sup:Suppliers)-[:SUPPLIES_BATCH]->(rmBatch:Batch {lotNumber: 'LOT-RM-2024-001'})
          -[:BATCH_FLOW*]->(endpoint:Distributor)
RETURN
  sup.companyName       AS Supplier,
  sup.iso3Code          AS SupplierCountry,
  [n IN nodes(p) WHERE n:Batch
    | n.lotNumber + ' [' + n.materialType + ']'] AS BatchLineage,
  endpoint.location     AS DistributedTo,
  endpoint.market       AS Market
----

=== Recall Impact Analysis

Given a raw material lot placed on quality hold, identify every downstream batch that used that material and every distribution point that received finished product derived from it. This is the "blast radius" of the hold decision - the complete set of nodes that may need to be quarantined, investigated, or recalled.

The `BATCH_FLOW*1..` pattern traverses an arbitrary number of hops, so the query works regardless of how many manufacturing stages exist between the raw material and the distributor.

[source, cypher, role=noheader]
----
// Determine the full downstream impact of a recalled raw material batch
MATCH (recalled:Batch {lotNumber: 'LOT-RM-2024-001'})
MATCH path = (recalled)-[:BATCH_FLOW*1..]->(affected)
WITH affected, length(path) AS depth
RETURN
  CASE
    WHEN affected:Batch     THEN affected.lotNumber
    WHEN affected:Distributor THEN affected.location
  END                                         AS AffectedNode,
  CASE
    WHEN affected:Batch     THEN affected.materialType
    WHEN affected:Distributor THEN 'Distributor'
  END                                         AS Stage,
  affected.location                           AS Location,
  depth                                       AS HopsFromRecall
ORDER BY depth, Stage
----

=== Equipment Traceability

For a given finished goods batch, identify every piece of equipment involved in its entire upstream manufacturing history. This information is required to scope any equipment-related CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action) following a batch failure or deviation.

[source, cypher, role=noheader]
----
// Identify all equipment used across the full production ancestry of a finished goods lot
MATCH (fg:Batch {lotNumber: 'LOT-FG-2024-001'})
MATCH (fg)<-[:BATCH_FLOW*0..]-(b:Batch)
      <-[:PRODUCED_BATCH]-(run:ProductionRun)
      -[:USED_EQUIP]->(equip:Equipment)
RETURN DISTINCT
  equip.nomenclature    AS EquipmentType,
  equip.manufacturer    AS Manufacturer,
  equip.model           AS Model,
  equip.serialNum       AS SerialNumber,
  equip.location        AS EquipmentSite,
  b.materialType        AS ProductionStage,
  b.lotNumber           AS BatchProducedHere
ORDER BY ProductionStage
----

=== Operator Involvement and Certification Audit

Identify every operator who performed a production run in the ancestry of a finished goods batch, and verify whether they held the required certification for the equipment they operated. This supports 21 CFR Part 11 / Annex 11 audit requirements and can surface compliance gaps where an operator ran equipment they were not yet certified on.

[source, cypher, role=noheader]
----
// Audit operator certifications across all production runs in a finished goods lot's ancestry
WITH 'LOT-FG-2024-001' AS targetLot
MATCH (fg:Batch {lotNumber: targetLot})
MATCH (fg)<-[:BATCH_FLOW*0..]-(b:Batch)
      <-[:PRODUCED_BATCH]-(run:ProductionRun)
MATCH (emp:Employee)-[:PERFORMED_RUN]->(run)
MATCH (run)-[:USED_EQUIP]->(equip:Equipment)
OPTIONAL MATCH (emp)-[cert:IS_CERTIFIED_OPERATOR]->(equip)
RETURN DISTINCT
  emp.firstName + ' ' + emp.lastName          AS Operator,
  emp.email                                   AS Email,
  b.materialType                              AS ProductionStage,
  equip.nomenclature                          AS Equipment,
  CASE
    WHEN cert IS NOT NULL THEN 'Certified'
    ELSE 'NOT CERTIFIED - Review Required'
  END                                         AS CertificationStatus,
  COALESCE(cert.yearsExperience, 0)           AS YearsExperience
ORDER BY ProductionStage, Operator
----

=== Single-Supplier Raw Material Risk

Identify raw materials that are sourced from only one supplier. These represent single points of failure: if that supplier experiences a quality issue, a geopolitical disruption, or a capacity constraint, there is no alternative source to fall back on. The Cardioxetin Base Compound is an example - it is exclusively supplied by SynthChem GmbH.

[source, cypher, role=noheader]
----
// Find raw materials with only one supplier - potential procurement bottlenecks
MATCH (rm:RM)<-[:SUPPLIES_RM]-(sup:Suppliers)
WITH rm,
     COUNT(DISTINCT sup)              AS supplierCount,
     COLLECT(DISTINCT sup.companyName) AS supplierNames
RETURN
  rm.globalBrand                      AS RawMaterial,
  rm.description                      AS Description,
  rm.location                         AS ProductionSite,
  supplierCount                       AS NumberOfSuppliers,
  supplierNames                       AS Suppliers,
  CASE
    WHEN supplierCount = 1
      THEN 'HIGH RISK - Single Supplier'
    ELSE 'Acceptable - Multiple Suppliers'
  END                                 AS RiskLevel
ORDER BY supplierCount ASC, rm.globalBrand
----

=== Cross-Batch Contamination Risk via Shared Equipment

Given a suspect batch, find all other batches that were produced on the same equipment. If the equipment was not cleaned to the required standard between runs (indicated by a shortened `postRunDuration`), those other batches may carry a contamination risk and should be included in the investigation scope.

This query also surfaces whether those other batches have already been distributed, giving the quality team an immediate indication of whether a market withdrawal may be required.

[source, cypher, role=noheader]
----
// Identify other batches produced on the same equipment as a suspect lot - cross-contamination risk
WITH 'LOT-BULK-2024-001' AS suspectLot
MATCH (suspect:Batch {lotNumber: suspectLot})
      <-[:PRODUCED_BATCH]-(suspectRun:ProductionRun)
      -[:USED_EQUIP]->(equip:Equipment)
MATCH (equip)<-[:USED_EQUIP]-(otherRun:ProductionRun)
      -[:PRODUCED_BATCH]->(otherBatch:Batch)
WHERE otherBatch.lotNumber <> suspectLot
// Check the cleaning window between the two runs
OPTIONAL MATCH (suspectRun)-[:POSTRUN]->(postRun:PostRunStage)
OPTIONAL MATCH (otherBatch)-[:BATCH_FLOW*]->(dist:Distributor)
RETURN
  equip.nomenclature                              AS SharedEquipment,
  equip.serialNum                                 AS SerialNumber,
  equip.location                                  AS EquipmentSite,
  otherBatch.lotNumber                            AS PotentiallyExposedBatch,
  otherBatch.materialType                         AS Stage,
  otherBatch.dateProduced                         AS DateProduced,
  COALESCE(postRun.postRunDuration, 'Unknown')    AS CleaningDurationAfterSuspectRun,
  COALESCE(dist.location, 'Not yet distributed')  AS DistributedTo
ORDER BY otherBatch.dateProduced
----

[TIP]
====
Extend this query to flag runs where the `postRunDuration` falls below a configurable minimum - for example 60 minutes - to automatically surface potential cleaning violations:

[source, cypher, role=noheader]
----
WITH 'LOT-BULK-2024-001' AS suspectLot,
     duration('PT60M')   AS minimumCleanDuration
MATCH (suspect:Batch {lotNumber: suspectLot})
      <-[:PRODUCED_BATCH]-(suspectRun:ProductionRun)
      -[:USED_EQUIP]->(equip:Equipment)
MATCH (suspectRun)-[:POSTRUN]->(postRun:PostRunStage)
RETURN
  equip.nomenclature                              AS Equipment,
  equip.serialNum                                 AS SerialNumber,
  postRun.postRunDuration                         AS ActualCleaningTime,
  minimumCleanDuration                            AS RequiredMinimum,
  CASE
    WHEN postRun.postRunDuration < minimumCleanDuration
      THEN 'VIOLATION - Insufficient Cleaning'
    ELSE 'Compliant'
  END                                             AS CleaningStatus
----

== Related Use Cases

The track-and-trace graph model is a foundation for several adjacent life-sciences use cases, each of which can be built on top of this dataset with minimal extensions.

* xref:dispensing-anomaly/dispensing-anomaly.adoc[Dispensing Anomaly and Fraud Detection] - add `Pharmacy`, `Prescription`, and `Prescriber` nodes to detect pharmacies dispensing at abnormal volumes, identify supply chain bypass routes, and cross-reference recalled batches with the pharmacies that received them.

* xref:counterfeit-diversion/counterfeit-diversion.adoc[Counterfeit and Diversion Detection] - add `SerialNumber` and `ScanEvent` nodes to implement unit-level chain-of-custody verification as required by DSCSA and FMD. Detect cloned serials, diverted units that skip mandatory scan points, and cold-chain timing violations.

* xref:adverse-event/adverse-event.adoc[Adverse Event Investigation] - add `AdverseEvent` and `CaseReport` nodes. A single graph traversal identifies the common upstream factor - shared equipment, operator, or raw-material supplier - linking multiple patient reports, and defines the recall blast radius.

* xref:equipment-utilisation/equipment-utilisation.adoc[Equipment Utilisation and Maintenance Planning] - no schema extension required. Compute run-to-idle ratios, flag below-standard cleaning windows, and match certified operators to under-utilised assets.

* xref:supplier-risk/supplier-risk.adoc[Supplier Risk and Resilience Scoring] - add `Country` nodes with geopolitical risk scores. Simulate supplier removal and identify which finished products would lose all upstream supply.
